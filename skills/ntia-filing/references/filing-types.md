# Filing types: waivers, reconsideration, letters, and rule text

Part of the ntia-filing skill. Read the section for the filing type you're drafting. Section numbers match the SKILL.md contents list.

## 7. Waivers

### Standard

- **General:** The Commission may waive a rule for good cause shown. 47 CFR § 1.3. Waiver is appropriate when special circumstances warrant a deviation from the general rule and the deviation serves the public interest better than strict adherence. *Northeast Cellular Tel. Co. v. FCC*, 897 F.2d 1164, 1166 (D.C. Cir. 1990); *see also WAIT Radio v. FCC*, 418 F.2d 1153, 1159 (D.C. Cir. 1969).
- **Wireless radio services:** Under 47 CFR § 1.925(b)(3), the petitioner must show either that (i) applying the rule would not serve, or would frustrate, its underlying purpose and a grant would serve the public interest, or (ii) unique or unusual facts make applying the rule inequitable, unduly burdensome, or contrary to the public interest, or the applicant has no reasonable alternative. Apply this standard to waivers of wireless radio service rules (for example, Parts 22, 24, 27, 90, 96, and 101); apply § 1.3 to others (for example, Parts 2, 15, and 25).

### Structure for comments on a waiver petition

1. Identify the petition, the spectrum rule (part and section), and the relief sought, with citations.
2. Describe the federal operations in or near the band.
3. State the position: no objection, conditional support, defer to a pending rulemaking, or opposition.
4. Tie the position to the standard. For example, the rule protects federal operations, so waiving it would frustrate its purpose; or the petitioner hasn't shown special circumstances, particularly when a pending rulemaking could provide the relief.
5. List the conditions NTIA requests, drafted per section 10 so the Commission can adopt them verbatim.

### Common conditions

- Operation on a non-interference basis, with no claim of protection from federal operations
- Coordination with NTIA and [agency] before operation
- Geographic, power, antenna, or time-of-use limits
- A 24/7 point of contact and immediate cessation on notice of harmful interference
- Reporting of deployment data
- A limited term
- No commercial operation until specified interference issues are resolved
- Grant without prejudice to [pending proceeding] and without precedential effect

## 8. Reconsideration, clarification, and stay

### Petition for reconsideration (47 CFR § 1.429)

- **Deadline:** within 30 days from the date of public notice of the action (47 CFR § 1.4(b)). For rulemakings, that's usually Federal Register publication. Verify the date.
- **Length:** 25 double-spaced pages or fewer. Oppositions: 15 days after public notice of the petition, 25 pages. Replies: 10 days after the opposition deadline, 10 pages.
- **Particularity:** State with particularity the respects in which the action should be changed. § 1.429(c).
- **New facts or arguments** are considered only if they reflect changed circumstances, were unknown and undiscoverable with ordinary diligence before the last opportunity to present them, or the Commission finds consideration required in the public interest. § 1.429(b). Label which ground applies.
- **Staff dismissal risk:** Staff may dismiss petitions that identify no material error or omission, repeat arguments already fully considered and rejected, lack particularity, exceed the order's scope, or are untimely. § 1.429(l). Draft to avoid each.
- **No automatic stay:** Filing doesn't stay the rule; request a stay separately. § 1.429(k).

**Structure:**
1. Relief requested: reconsider, or in the alternative clarify, and the specific changes sought (rescind, modify, add conditions).
2. Summary of grounds.
3. Each ground as its own section: the order's holding with pin cite, the material error or omission, the record evidence the order overlooked or misread, and the change requested.
4. Procedural errors, if any, in a separate section.
5. Conclusion restating the relief.

### Petition for stay

Address all four factors in order, with evidence for each. Irreparable harm needs concrete, specific, and imminent harm to identified federal operations, supported by agency declarations or technical analysis. Likelihood of success should cross-reference the reconsideration petition's strongest ground. Flag any factor the input doesn't support.

### Clarification

When the order is ambiguous, ask the Commission to confirm a specific reading. Quote the ambiguous text, give NTIA's reading, and explain the operational consequence of the other reading.

## 9. Letters

### Letter to the Chairman or a Commissioner

```
<Date>

The Honorable <Name>
Chairman
Federal Communications Commission
45 L Street NE
Washington, DC 20554

Re: <Proceeding name>, <Docket type> Docket No. <XX-XXX>

Dear Chairman <Name>:

<Opening: NTIA, on behalf of the Executive Branch, writes regarding / submits the enclosed materials for consideration in the above-referenced proceeding.>

<Body paragraphs using section 4.>

<Close: NTIA appreciates the Commission's attention to these matters and looks forward to continued coordination.>

Sincerely,

<Name>
Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Communications and Information

Enclosures (if any)
cc: <Commissioners or bureau chiefs, if any>
```

### Staff-level letter

From the OSM Associate Administrator to a bureau or office chief (for example, the Chief of the Office of Engineering and Technology). Use "Dear <Title> <Name>:", band-by-band or issue-by-issue headings, and numbered enclosures. Tone is technical; positions stay within what the Executive Branch has cleared.

### Pre-vote letter

Sent before a scheduled Commission vote. Open by noting the pending item and NTIA's support where it exists, then state the federal equities and any requested changes. Keep it short and specific so it can shape the final text.

### Ex parte notice

Address it to the FCC Secretary with "Re: <Proceeding>, <Docket No.>; Notice of Ex Parte Presentation." State the date, the participants on both sides, and a summary of every data point and argument presented, as 47 CFR § 1.1206(b) requires. Flag whether a federal-agency exemption under 47 CFR § 1.1204(a)(5) applies before omitting a notice; default to filing one.

### Transmittal of materials

Open with NTIA submitting the enclosed materials for the Commission's consideration in the proceeding, describe each enclosure in one sentence, and state the Executive Branch position the materials support. When enclosing letters from other agencies, cite each one in full ("Letter from <Name>, <Title>, <Agency>, to <Name>, Chairman, FCC (<date>) (Enclosure 2)").

## 10. Proposed rule text and conditions

Draft rule text only for spectrum-related CFR parts (section 1). Follow the Office of the Federal Register's Document Drafting Handbook (DDH) and its plain-language conventions. Verify against the current DDH edition and the current eCFR text of the section being amended before finalizing.

- Use *must* for requirements, *must not* for prohibitions, and *may* for permissions. Don't use *shall*.
- Avoid *herein*, *hereinafter*, *thereunder*, *such* as a pronoun, *above*, *below*, *e.g.*, and *i.e.* Write "in this section," "in this paragraph," and "for example."
- Put the general rule before exceptions. Don't put operative requirements inside definitions.
- Use the CFR paragraph hierarchy: (a), (1), (i), (A).
- For amendments, write amendatory instructions with the standard DDH verbs (for example, add, remove, revise, redesignate, and revise and republish) and give the full text of each changed paragraph.
- For Table of Frequency Allocations changes (§ 2.106), match the table's existing format for allocations and footnotes (US, NG, G, and international footnotes).
- For license and waiver conditions in a filing, number each condition and write each as one enforceable sentence that names who must act, what they must do, and when. Name the right actor: *the licensee* for license conditions, *the grantee* or *the petitioner* for waiver conditions, *non-federal operators* for band-wide rules.

**Example conditions:**
> (1) The licensee must coordinate each base station within the coordination zones published by NTIA before commencing operation.
>
> (2) The grantee must cease operation of any station immediately upon notice from NTIA or the Commission that the station is causing harmful interference to federal operations.
