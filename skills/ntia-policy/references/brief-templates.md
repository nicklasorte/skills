# Brief templates

Use these templates in Draft mode. They follow memo conventions: a TO/FROM/DATE/RE header, third-person address of the principal, and organizational *we*. Fill every bracketed field from the inputs, or leave it as `[CONFIRM: <field>]`.

## Decision memo

Use when the principal must choose among options.

```
DRAFT - PRE-DECISIONAL AND DELIBERATIVE
Prepared with AI assistance. Reviewer: [CONFIRM]
Rule text as of: <YYYY-MM-DD>

TO:    [CONFIRM: principal name and title]
FROM:  [CONFIRM: office]
DATE:  <Month D, YYYY>
RE:    <Issue in a short noun phrase>

DECISION REQUESTED
<One sentence: what the principal must decide, and by when if there's a deadline.>

BOTTOM LINE
<Two or three sentences: the situation, the main tradeoff, and the recommendation if one was supplied.>

BACKGROUND
<The facts the principal needs, in three to six short paragraphs. Lead with the rule or event that created the issue. Cite sources in footnotes.>

FEDERAL EQUITIES
<Agencies, systems, bands, or programs affected, from public sources only. Write "None identified" if there are none.>

OPTIONS
Option 1: <Short name>
  What it does: <One or two sentences.>
  Benefits: <...>
  Costs and risks: <...>
  Who must act: <NTIA, IRAC, FCC, Congress, or another agency>
Option 2: <...>
Option 3: <...>

RECOMMENDATION
<The recommended option and the main reason, or [CONFIRM: recommendation].>

COORDINATION
<Offices and agencies that have cleared or must clear, or [CONFIRM: clearance].>

DECISION
____ Approve Option 1   ____ Approve Option 2   ____ Approve Option 3   ____ Discuss

ATTACHMENTS
<List, or "None.">
```

Guidance:

- Keep the memo to two pages when you can. Move detail into attachments.
- Make options mutually exclusive and comparable. Include "retain the current rule" or "no action" when it's viable.
- Put benefits and costs in the same terms across options, so the principal can compare them.
- Leave policy judgments to the principal. Describe tradeoffs; don't argue for a political outcome.

## Information memo

Use when the principal needs to know something but doesn't need to decide.

```
DRAFT - PRE-DECISIONAL AND DELIBERATIVE
Prepared with AI assistance. Reviewer: [CONFIRM]
Rule text as of: <YYYY-MM-DD>

TO:    [CONFIRM: principal name and title]
FROM:  [CONFIRM: office]
DATE:  <Month D, YYYY>
RE:    <Issue in a short noun phrase>

PURPOSE
<One sentence: why the principal is getting this memo.>

BOTTOM LINE
<Two or three sentences.>

DISCUSSION
<The facts, in short paragraphs or a short list.>

NEXT STEPS
<What happens next, who does it, and when.>
```

## One-page brief

Use for a meeting, a call, or a briefing book entry.

```
<Issue title>
As of: <Month D, YYYY>

Bottom line
- <Three bullets or fewer.>

Background
- <Four bullets or fewer, each one fact.>

Key numbers
- <Only numbers from the sources, each cited.>

If asked
- <Likely questions, each with a short, factual answer.>

Sources
- <Full citations.>
```

## Footnotes and sources

- Use numbered footnotes in memos and a "Sources" list in one-page briefs.
- Use the citation formats in `references/sources.md`.
- Every number in the memo needs a footnote, unless the user supplied it as their own analysis. Label the user's analysis as such.
